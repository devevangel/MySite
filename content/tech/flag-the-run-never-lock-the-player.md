The first version of "something looks wrong" was a lock. Enough mismatches, and paid challenges closed for that account until a person cleared them.

That felt firm. It was also the wrong firm.

A weak phone, a bad night of sleep, a browser that rounded a number differently — those can look like a mismatch. Locking the start button turns a maybe into a punishment. The player did not get the reward. They also cannot try again. That is how you lose honest people and keep the ones who will open a second account.

## The rule that replaced it

Integrity **pauses the reward. It does not block play.**

A live signal that fires makes the session yellow. The reward waits. The run can still be recorded. The next start still works. A kill switch can stop automatic credits without closing the game.

New accounts still need a little verified free history before they can pay to enter. That is a ramp, not a strike. It is about "have you ever finished a real run," not "did a detector dislike you."

## Watch first, then trust

A new check starts in the background. It writes a reason a person can read. It does not pause a reward until it has earned that.

"Earned" means reviewers have tagged enough of those flags as real or as false. If too many were false, the check stays in the background. If the sample is empty, it stays in the background. A job can promote it later. A person can put it back.

That is the same idea as the review queue I already wrote about — impossibilities fail on their own, maybes wait for a person — with one extra step: even the maybe does not get to lock the door.

## What I would tell my earlier self

The start button is not a lever for suspicion. The balance is. If you mix them, you will spend your review time on people who just wanted to play, and you will teach the next cheater to open a new login.
